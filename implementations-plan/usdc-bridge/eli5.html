Galactica Bridge Plan

We will bring back the simple USDC bridge from nulo's history and rebuild it cleanly in this repo. When the plan is done, a user can move USDC from Ethereum (Sepolia) onto Aztec, publicly or privately, and move it back. A new React web app and freshly deployed contracts on the Aztec testnet deliver that.

How the bridge works, in one picture

Ethereum (L1)

Your USDC is locked in the portal contract. You sign once with Permit2 to authorize each deposit.

deposit → ← withdraw
Aztec (L2)

The bridge mints the same amount of USDC to your Aztec account. Going back burns it on Aztec and releases it on Ethereum.

Deposits take a few minutes to become claimable on Aztec. Withdrawals wait for Aztec to prove the block, which can take up to about an hour on testnet.

Public vs private. Public mode puts your Aztec address on Ethereum. Private mode hides who receives on Aztec: the recipient is sealed inside a secret that only your browser knows. Private mode does not hide how much or when; amounts and timing are visible on both chains.

Risks you chose to accept

Closing the tab mid-deposit loses that deposit

Nothing is saved to disk. If you close the tab after sending USDC on Ethereum but before claiming on Aztec, the claim secret is gone and that USDC stays locked forever. The app warns you before closing. Withdrawals are not affected: you can finish one later with the Aztec transaction ID plus the address and amount you chose.

Permit2 unlimited approval

The first deposit gives Permit2 permission to move all of your USDC. After that, if any website tricks you into signing a Permit2 message for someone else, your whole USDC balance can be taken. This is how Permit2 normally works, and the app says so at the approval step. USDC's own permit was the safer option and was declined.

Private-mode fee privacy depends on the wallet

For private claims and exits the app asks the wallet to pay fees through a shared sponsor, so your account isn't linked. Each wallet decides whether to honor that. Only the test wallet is proven. If sponsorship fails, the app asks before falling back.

Pausing claims can cost in-flight deposits

You kept V1's pause, which stops claims as well as withdrawals. Since nothing is saved, anyone with an unclaimed deposit when you pause must keep their tab open until you unpause. The app refuses new deposits while paused and says so on screen.

What we took from V2's testing

Three searches compared V2's tests with V1's code. The plain summary:

  • Aztec side: no bugs found. V1's code already enforces every rule V2 tests; 14 of those rules simply had no test. They now do, along with V2's stricter tooling.
  • Ethereum side: one real gap. V1's portal trusted the router to check amounts, but anyone can call the portal directly. It now checks for itself (you approved this).
  • App side: V1 had no real end-to-end tests at all. We adopt V2's harness plus its failure drills.
  • Left behind: tests for V2-only features (the multi-token factory, fuel swaps, the faucet, saved history).

What the reviewers caught, and what changed

  • The old deploy method let anyone grab ownership of the new Aztec contracts first, including the pause switch. Contracts are now tied to our deploy account.
  • Testnet compatibility was only going to be proven at the very end. It is now checked on day one and again in chunk 2, before any UI work.
  • A slow network response could drop your deposit secret. It is now saved in memory before you sign.
  • "Private" was over-promised. The copy now says it hides who, not how much or when.
  • Withdrawals assumed the first message in a transaction was ours. They now look for the exact message.
  • The app would have leaked your IP to a public Ethereum node. It now reads Ethereum only through your own wallet.
  • Recovering a private withdrawal from the transaction ID alone is impossible, because the chain only stores a fingerprint of the address and amount. The recovery form now asks for both.
  • If the security audit changes a contract after the testnet deploy, the plan now forces a redeploy and a fresh smoke test before any PR.
  • The portal's new payout check would have trapped any withdrawal addressed to the portal itself. The app now refuses that address before anything is burned.
  • The "committed contracts match the source" check could miss a renamed function. It now also compares the function list, not just the contract fingerprint.
